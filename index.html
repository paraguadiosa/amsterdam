Read the Amsterdam sonnet

Upon the Amstel's bank they raised a dam,
and round it grew a city, stone on stone,
whose gabled houses lean above the stream
and hold a thousand bridges of their own.

So here we build a dam of a newer kind —
a little page that holds a river wide;
the tokens of a hundred models wind
through its canals, and none may slip the tide.

No coin is minted here, no key is kept;
the consoles open like the morning light.
The balance in the water, softly swept,
is yours to read by day, to guard by night.

So let the river run, and let it flow —
the dam holds fast what you most need to know.
